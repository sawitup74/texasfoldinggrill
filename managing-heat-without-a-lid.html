Managing Heat Without a Lid

How to cook Texas-style on an open flat pack grill: coal placement, fuel feeding, foil, pans and Dutch ovens in place of a lid.

A flat pack grill like the Slot Grill has no lid, so you can't trap heat the way an offset smoker or kettle does. That's not a flaw for grilling, where open heat is exactly right. For slower cooks, you borrow tools.

Your heat controls

  • Distance: food over coals vs beside them.
  • Quantity: how many coals are lit.
  • Spread: piled tight is hotter, spread thin is cooler.
  • Feeding: adding lit coals keeps a steady temperature over hours.
  • Containment: foil wrap, a foil pan or a cast iron Dutch oven holds moisture and heat around tougher cuts.

Smoke first, then contain

Smoke flavor mostly goes on early, while meat is cold and its surface is moist. So for ribs, brisket flats or chuck, smoke uncovered on the cool side for the first couple of hours, then wrap in foil or move into a Dutch oven to finish tender. That's the method behind brisket flat on a small grill and ribs on a flat pack grill.

Be honest about scale

A whole packer brisket on a small lidless grill is not a good plan. Smaller cuts, sections of ribs, links, steaks, fajitas and birds are where a flat pack grill shines.

Safe cooking, Texas edition

  • Check the burn ban for the county every trip; see burn bans.
  • Bare dirt, gravel, sand or concrete. Never dry grass, a truck bed, a tailgate or a boat deck.
  • Outdoors only. Charcoal makes carbon monoxide; never use it in a tent, camper, garage, vehicle or boat cabin.
  • Water and a shovel within reach; gloves for hot steel.
  • Drown and stir ash until cold before you leave.

Questions people ask

Can you smoke meat on a grill with no lid?

You can add real smoke flavor on the cool side of a lidless grill, then finish tough cuts wrapped or in a Dutch oven. It isn't the same as a closed smoker.
