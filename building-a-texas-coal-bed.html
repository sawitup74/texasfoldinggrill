Building a Texas Coal Bed in a Folding Grill

How to light and lay out charcoal and wood coals in a small folding grill for searing, fajitas and slower beef cooks.

Everything on a lidless grill starts with how you lay the coals. Here are the four layouts Texas cooks use most on a small folding grill.

LayoutHowFor
Full bedCoals evenly across the fireboxFajitas, smash burgers, quick steaks, sausage browning
Two-zoneCoals on two-thirds, last third emptySteaks, chicken, links: sear then finish
BankedCoals piled at one end only, food at the otherIndirect cooking: ribs sections, brisket flat stage one
Ring of fireCoals around the edges, empty middleA Dutch oven or a pan you want hot from the sides

Lighting

  1. Light lump charcoal in a chimney with paper or natural fire starters; skip lighter fluid.
  2. Wait for gray ash on the top coals, 15 to 20 minutes.
  3. Pour into your layout with gloves.
  4. Add a wood chunk if you want smoke.
  5. Set the grates and preheat a few minutes.

How hot is it?

The old hand test: hold your palm a few inches above the grate (carefully) and count. Pulling away in 2 seconds is very hot; 4 to 5 seconds is medium; 6 to 8 is low. It's rough, but it works with no gadgets. For meat, always trust an instant-read thermometer.

Fire plan for a small Texas cook

CookFireFuel
Fajitas, steaks, smash burgersFull or two-zone, hotLump charcoal, mesquite chunk
Links, chickenTwo-zone, mediumCharcoal plus oak or pecan
Ribs, brisket flat (smoke stage)Banked at one endCharcoal plus post oak, fed every 30–45 min
Dutch oven braiseSpread, moderateSteady charcoal, fed as needed

Safe cooking, Texas edition

  • Check the burn ban for the county every trip; see burn bans.
  • Bare dirt, gravel, sand or concrete. Never dry grass, a truck bed, a tailgate or a boat deck.
  • Outdoors only. Charcoal makes carbon monoxide; never use it in a tent, camper, garage, vehicle or boat cabin.
  • Water and a shovel within reach; gloves for hot steel.
  • Drown and stir ash until cold before you leave.
