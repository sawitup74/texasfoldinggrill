Post Oak, Mesquite and Hickory on a Small Grill

Which Texas woods to burn in a small folding charcoal grill, how each one tastes, and how to use chunks and splits without bitter smoke.

Central Texas barbecue runs on post oak. South Texas and the border lean on mesquite. East Texas often uses hickory and pecan. All of them work in a small folding grill; the trick is scale. A firebox the size of a Slot Grill Compact or Full Size wants small pieces, burned down to coals, not log-sized splits throwing flames.

WoodFlavorHow to use it on a small grill

MesquiteStrong, earthy, burns hotGreat for fast grilling (fajitas, steaks); go easy on long cooks, where it can turn harsh

Charcoal base, wood for flavor

The easiest Texas method on a small grill: a bed of lit lump charcoal for steady heat, and one fist-sized chunk of oak or hickory on the coals for smoke. Add another chunk when the smoke fades. You want thin, pale blue smoke. Thick white smoke means the wood is smoldering; give it more air or a smaller piece.

All wood

Both Slot Grill models burn cooking wood as well as charcoal. Burn splits down to coals first; see burning splits down to coals.

Source it locally

Buy or gather firewood close to where you burn it, use only dry, untreated hardwood, and follow any firewood rules at parks and leases.

Safe cooking, Texas edition

  • Check the burn ban for the county every trip; see burn bans.
  • Bare dirt, gravel, sand or concrete. Never dry grass, a truck bed, a tailgate or a boat deck.
  • Outdoors only. Charcoal makes carbon monoxide; never use it in a tent, camper, garage, vehicle or boat cabin.
  • Water and a shovel within reach; gloves for hot steel.
  • Drown and stir ash until cold before you leave.

Questions people ask

Is mesquite too strong for barbecue?

Mesquite is excellent for fast, hot grilling. On long cooks it can get harsh, so many cooks use it sparingly or mix it with oak.
