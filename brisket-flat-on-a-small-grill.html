Brisket Flat on a Small Folding Grill: Smoke, Then Braise

An honest small-grill brisket method: smoke a brisket flat on the cool side of a folding charcoal grill, then finish tender in a Dutch oven.

A whole packer brisket and a 14-hour fire are not what a small, lidless folding grill is for. A brisket flat (3 to 5 lb) is. This method gets real smoke flavor on the grill, then finishes the beef tender in a covered cast iron Dutch oven on the same fire. It isn't a pit brisket, and we won't pretend it is, but it's a very good one, and it works at a lease or a campsite. Ingredients

  • 1 brisket flat, 3–5 lb, fat cap trimmed to about ¼"
  • Texas salt and pepper rub (see link below)
  • 1 cup beef broth
  • Lump charcoal plus 3–4 chunks of post oak
  • A cast iron Dutch oven large enough to hold the flat (or heavy foil)

Method

  1. Season the brisket heavily the night before or at least an hour ahead.
  2. Build a banked fire: lit coals at one end only. Add a wood chunk.
  3. Set the brisket fat-side up on the cool end, as far from the coals as it'll go. Keep thin blue smoke going, adding a few lit coals and a chunk of wood every 30 to 45 minutes.
  4. Smoke 2 to 3 hours, until the bark is dark and set and the internal temperature is around 160–170°F.
  5. Move the brisket into the Dutch oven with the broth (or wrap tightly in foil with a splash of broth). Cover.
  6. Set the Dutch oven over a moderate, spread-out bed of coals (not a roaring pile). Keep it gently simmering, adding lit coals as needed.
  7. Cook until probe-tender: a thermometer probe slides in like into soft butter, usually around 200–205°F, roughly 2 to 3 more hours.
  8. Rest, covered, at least 30 minutes (an hour is better) in a cooler with towels.
  9. Slice across the grain about the thickness of a pencil. Spoon juices over.

Tips

  • Brisket is safe long before it's tender; 145°F is the safe minimum for whole beef, but it needs 200°F-plus to break down.
  • Check the coals under the Dutch oven every 30 minutes; you want a simmer, not a boil.
  • Leftovers make great chopped beef sandwiches.

Safe internal temperatures

Check the thickest part with an instant-read thermometer, away from bone. These are USDA minimum safe temperatures; tougher cuts like brisket and ribs are cooked well past them for tenderness.

FoodMinimum internal temperature
Beef, pork, lamb and veal steaks, chops and roasts145°F, then rest 3 minutes
Ground beef, pork, lamb and veal (burgers, fresh sausage)160°F
Chicken and turkey, whole, pieces or ground165°F
Fish and shellfish145°F, or flesh opaque and flakes with a fork
Egg dishes160°F; cook eggs until yolks and whites are firm
Precooked sausage, hot dogs and leftoversReheat to 165°F

Questions people ask

Can I cook a whole packer brisket on a small grill?

It's not a good fit for a small lidless grill. A 3–5 lb flat with a smoke-then-braise finish is the practical approach.
