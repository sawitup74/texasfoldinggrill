Sides for a Texas BBQ Plate

The classic Texas barbecue plate and sides you can make at camp: pinto beans, slaw, potato salad, pickles, onions and white bread.

A Texas barbecue plate is meat on butcher paper with simple sides. Most can be made ahead; a couple can cook on the grill.

SideMake-ahead or on the grill
Pickles, raw onion, white bread or saltinesNo cooking
Pinto beansSimmer in a Dutch oven on the cool side, or reheat to 165°F
ColeslawMake ahead; keep cold
Potato saladMake ahead; keep at 40°F or below
Grilled jalapeñosOn the grill, a few minutes until blistered
Elote (street corn)On the grill; see grilled street corn

Cold sides, cold cooler

Mayonnaise-based salads must stay cold. Keep them in a separate cooler or deep in ice, and don't leave them out more than 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F).

Fire plan for a small Texas cook

CookFireFuel
Fajitas, steaks, smash burgersFull or two-zone, hotLump charcoal, mesquite chunk
Links, chickenTwo-zone, mediumCharcoal plus oak or pecan
Ribs, brisket flat (smoke stage)Banked at one endCharcoal plus post oak, fed every 30–45 min
Dutch oven braiseSpread, moderateSteady charcoal, fed as needed

Cooler rules for hot Texas days

  • Keep it at 40°F or below; refill ice before it's gone.
  • Raw meat sealed, on the bottom; drinks in a separate cooler so the food lid stays shut.
  • Perishables out no more than 1 hour above 90°F.
  • Separate tongs and platters for raw and cooked.

Look after the steel

Carbon steel and Gulf humidity don't mix unless you keep it oiled. Scrape warm, empty cold ash, wipe the cooking surfaces with a thin coat of oil or Seasoning Balm, and store it dry. More in care in Texas humidity.
