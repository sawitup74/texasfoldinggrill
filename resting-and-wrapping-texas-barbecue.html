Resting and Wrapping Texas Barbecue

Butcher paper, foil and the cooler rest: how wrapping and resting help small-grill barbecue, and how to do both safely.

Two steps separate tender barbecue from tough barbecue: wrapping during the cook and resting afterward.

Wrapping

WrapEffectBest for on a small grill
FoilTraps steam; fastest, softest; softens barkRibs and brisket flat (the practical choice on an open grill)
Butcher paperBreathes; keeps more barkWorks but cooks slower; better when you can hold steady heat
Dutch ovenLike foil with a lid; great on a lidless grillBrisket flat, short ribs, chuck

Resting

  • Rest beef ribs and brisket at least 30 minutes, wrapped, in a dry cooler with towels.
  • Steaks and chops: 3 to 5 minutes.
  • A long rest keeps meat hot, but keep it above 140°F if holding for hours, and refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Fire plan for a small Texas cook

CookFireFuel
Fajitas, steaks, smash burgersFull or two-zone, hotLump charcoal, mesquite chunk
Links, chickenTwo-zone, mediumCharcoal plus oak or pecan
Ribs, brisket flat (smoke stage)Banked at one endCharcoal plus post oak, fed every 30–45 min
Dutch oven braiseSpread, moderateSteady charcoal, fed as needed

Cooler rules for hot Texas days

  • Keep it at 40°F or below; refill ice before it's gone.
  • Raw meat sealed, on the bottom; drinks in a separate cooler so the food lid stays shut.
  • Perishables out no more than 1 hour above 90°F.
  • Separate tongs and platters for raw and cooked.

Look after the steel

Carbon steel and Gulf humidity don't mix unless you keep it oiled. Scrape warm, empty cold ash, wipe the cooking surfaces with a thin coat of oil or Seasoning Balm, and store it dry. More in care in Texas humidity.
