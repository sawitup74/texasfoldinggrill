Burn Bans and Fire Rules for Grilling in Texas

How Texas county burn bans and park rules can affect charcoal grilling, and how to check before you light.

Texas counties can issue burn bans in dry conditions, and parks, lakes and beaches set their own fire rules. Whether a charcoal grill is allowed during a ban depends on the county order and the location. Rules change with the weather, so check every trip.

How to check

  • Look up the current burn ban status for your county (the Texas A&M Forest Service publishes a statewide burn ban map).
  • Read the county order. Many separate outdoor burning from cooking in a grill with a lid or enclosure; some don't. Don't guess.
  • Check park, lake, river outfitter or beach rules.
  • On private land and leases, ask the landowner.

Good habits on dry days

  • Grill on bare dirt, gravel, sand or concrete, never dry grass.
  • Keep water and a shovel handy.
  • Skip it on red-flag, high-wind days.
  • Drown ash and stir until cold.

Safe cooking, Texas edition

  • Check the burn ban for the county every trip; see burn bans.
  • Bare dirt, gravel, sand or concrete. Never dry grass, a truck bed, a tailgate or a boat deck.
  • Outdoors only. Charcoal makes carbon monoxide; never use it in a tent, camper, garage, vehicle or boat cabin.
  • Water and a shovel within reach; gloves for hot steel.
  • Drown and stir ash until cold before you leave.
