Pork Spare Ribs on a Folding Grill

Cooking pork ribs on a small lidless charcoal grill by cutting racks into sections, smoking indirect, then wrapping to finish tender.

A full rack of spare ribs won't sit away from the coals on a small grill. Cut it into 3- or 4-bone sections and it will. Smoke on the cool side, wrap to finish, and glaze at the end if you like it sticky.

Serves 3–4Time 4–5 hoursGrill Slot Grill Full Size

Ingredients

  • 1 rack pork spare ribs (or St. Louis-cut), membrane removed
  • Rub: 2 tbsp each salt and coarse pepper, 1 tbsp paprika, 1 tbsp brown sugar, 1 tsp garlic powder
  • Splash of apple juice or broth
  • Barbecue sauce (optional)
  • Heavy-duty foil

Method

  1. Cut the rack into 3–4 bone sections. Season all over.
  2. Build a banked fire at one end with a hickory or pecan chunk.
  3. Arrange rib sections bone-side down on the cool end. Keep thin smoke and a steady low fire, adding a few lit coals every 30 to 45 minutes.
  4. Smoke about 2 to 2½ hours, rotating sections so each takes a turn closest to the heat, until the bark is set.
  5. Wrap each section (or pairs) in foil with a splash of juice. Return to the cool side, 1 to 1½ hours.
  6. Check tenderness: a probe should slide between bones easily, typically around 195–203°F.
  7. Optional: unwrap, brush with sauce and set over the edge of the coals for a few minutes to tack up.
  8. Rest 10 minutes, slice between the bones.

Tips

  • Ribs are safe at 145°F but need far higher temperatures to get tender.
  • Sauce burns quickly over direct heat; watch it.

Safe internal temperatures

Check the thickest part with an instant-read thermometer, away from bone. These are USDA minimum safe temperatures; tougher cuts like brisket and ribs are cooked well past them for tenderness.

Beef, pork, lamb and veal steaks, chops and roasts145°F, then rest 3 minutes

Cooler rules for hot Texas days

  • Keep it at 40°F or below; refill ice before it's gone.
  • Raw meat sealed, on the bottom; drinks in a separate cooler so the food lid stays shut.
  • Perishables out no more than 1 hour above 90°F.
  • Separate tongs and platters for raw and cooked.

Look after the steel

Carbon steel and Gulf humidity don't mix unless you keep it oiled. Scrape warm, empty cold ash, wipe the cooking surfaces with a thin coat of oil or Seasoning Balm, and store it dry. More in care in Texas humidity.

Questions people ask

When are ribs done?

When a probe slides between the bones easily, usually around 195–203°F. They're safe at 145°F but not yet tender.
