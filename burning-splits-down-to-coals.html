Burning Splits Down to Coals

How to cook on wood coals in a small folding grill: split size, the burn-down, and keeping fresh coals coming.

Cooking on pure wood coals is old-school Texas, and a folding steel grill handles it fine; Slot Grill says both models burn charcoal or cooking wood.

  1. Split wood small: thumb to wrist thick, short enough to lie in the firebox.
  2. Start a small fire with kindling.
  3. Add splits a couple at a time. Let flames burn down.
  4. After roughly 30 to 45 minutes you'll have a bed of glowing coals.
  5. Rake them where you need heat; keep one split burning at the far end as a coal factory.
  6. Rake new coals over as the bed fades.

Tips

  • Dry wood only; wet wood smokes and sputters.
  • Don't cook over tall flames. You'll get soot and char, not barbecue.
  • Mesquite coals run very hot; great for fajitas.

Safe cooking, Texas edition

  • Check the burn ban for the county every trip; see burn bans.
  • Bare dirt, gravel, sand or concrete. Never dry grass, a truck bed, a tailgate or a boat deck.
  • Outdoors only. Charcoal makes carbon monoxide; never use it in a tent, camper, garage, vehicle or boat cabin.
  • Water and a shovel within reach; gloves for hot steel.
  • Drown and stir ash until cold before you leave.
