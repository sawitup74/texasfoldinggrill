Lake Weekend Grilling in Texas

Using a folding charcoal grill at Texas lakes: where to set up, boat safety, food for a lake day, and cleanup.

From the Highland Lakes to the East Texas reservoirs, a lake weekend means cooking on shore. A flat-pack grill rides in the boat as cargo and comes out on the bank.

Rules that matter

  • Never light a charcoal grill on a boat deck or near the boat. Slot Grill's FAQ says the same: carry it as cargo and cook on shore.
  • Check park and lake authority rules for fires and grills.
  • Cook on sand, gravel or bare dirt, away from dry vegetation.

Lake-day menu

  • Fish on the half shell (works for many lake fish too)
  • Fajitas on the griddle
  • Grilled corn

Cleanup

Let ash cool, drown it if allowed, pack it out in a metal can. Wipe and oil the grill once home; lake humidity is hard on bare carbon steel.

Safe cooking, Texas edition

  • Check the burn ban for the county every trip; see burn bans.
  • Bare dirt, gravel, sand or concrete. Never dry grass, a truck bed, a tailgate or a boat deck.
  • Outdoors only. Charcoal makes carbon monoxide; never use it in a tent, camper, garage, vehicle or boat cabin.
  • Water and a shovel within reach; gloves for hot steel.
  • Drown and stir ash until cold before you leave.

Cooler rules for hot Texas days

  • Keep it at 40°F or below; refill ice before it's gone.
  • Raw meat sealed, on the bottom; drinks in a separate cooler so the food lid stays shut.
  • Perishables out no more than 1 hour above 90°F.
  • Separate tongs and platters for raw and cooked.

Look after the steel

Carbon steel and Gulf humidity don't mix unless you keep it oiled. Scrape warm, empty cold ash, wipe the cooking surfaces with a thin coat of oil or Seasoning Balm, and store it dry. More in care in Texas humidity.

Questions people ask

Can I grill on my boat?

Not with charcoal. Carry the grill as cargo and cook on shore.
